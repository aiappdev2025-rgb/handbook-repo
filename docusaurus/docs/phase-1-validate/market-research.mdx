## Why Market Research First?

Many products fail not because they're poorly built, but because they solve problems nobody has or compete in markets too crowded to enter. Research forces you to gather evidence before committing resources.

:::warning The Costly Mistake
Skipping market research is the most expensive mistake you can make. Building a product nobody wants costs months of work and thousands of dollars.
:::

## Research Areas

Your market research should cover five key areas:

### 1. Market Size (TAM/SAM/SOM)

- **TAM (Total Addressable Market)**: The entire market for your type of solution
- **SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)**: The portion you can realistically reach
- **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)**: What you can capture in year one

### 2. Competitive Landscape

- Who are the major players?
- What are their strengths and weaknesses?
- How are they priced?
- Where are the gaps in the market?

### 3. Customer Pain Points

- What problems remain unsolved or poorly solved?
- What do customers complain about in existing solutions?
- What workarounds are people using?

### 4. Market Trends

- Where is this market heading in 2-3 years?
- What technology shifts are happening?
- Are there regulatory changes coming?

### 5. Barriers to Entry

- What makes this market hard to enter?
- Capital requirements?
- Technical expertise?
- Network effects?

## How to Validate Your Research

Before proceeding, ensure your research passes these quality checks:

| Criterion | What Good Looks Like |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Market Size | Specific numbers with credible sources (Gartner, Statista, industry reports) |
| Competitors | At least 5 named competitors with positioning analysis |
| Pain Points | Validated by real user feedback (reviews, forums, surveys) |
| Differentiator | Clear angle that isn't easily copied |
| Barriers | No fatal barriers discovered |
